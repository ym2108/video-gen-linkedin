A good idea can be lost without anyone saying “no.”
It arrives from a supplier. The product team sees potential. Quality needs more evidence. Finance wants a forecast. Months pass, and the idea is still “under review.”
A.G. Lafley challenged this pattern at P&G. Through Connect + Develop, the company looked beyond its own walls for ideas it could develop and bring to market. P&G’s Pringles Prints became a notable example: its innovation leaders said it reached market in less than a year, versus an estimated two years through the old approach.
The lesson for a CEO is practical: opening the door to outside ideas only helps if someone owns the next decision.
Give each promising idea a named owner, a short test window and a date to decide: advance, revise or stop.
Otherwise, even the best suggestion can disappear between departments.
Where does a promising idea most often get stuck in your organisation: getting noticed, getting tested, or getting approved?
